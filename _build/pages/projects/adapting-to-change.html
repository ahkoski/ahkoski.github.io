The challenge

Employees were navigating a steady stream of changes to tools, processes, and expectations. Change-management content often stays abstract: people can name a model but don’t know what to do on a hard day. The goal was a course that made the ideas personal and gave people room to practice their responses before they needed them.

My approach

Teach the models, then make them usable

The course introduces Bridges’ Transition Model and the Kübler-Ross Change Curve, then moves straight into practical tools: Circles of Control (what I control, what I can influence, and what’s outside my control), stress management (a deep-breath exercise, reframing, and a growth mindset), the importance of a plan, common change-management models, and communication strategies.

Practice in the Arcade

Learners enter the Arcade, a set of five mini-games that turn the content into decisions. In a racetrack game, they work through rounds of realistic moments, like “You hear conflicting information about an upcoming change. What do you do next?”, and choose the most helpful response for where they are in the transition. Other games use drag-and-drop sorting and pick-one choices built around everyday scenarios, such as a new scheduling tool rollout or changes to team availability expectations.

Designed so playing reinforces learning

The game rounds follow the stages of transition, so practicing the game rehearses the model. Immediate feedback, results screens, and replayable rounds keep learners active instead of clicking through.

Results

  • 1,430 completions of an optional course, with only an 8% abandon rate.
  • Pilot reviewers rated the course “Very Effective” and said it struck a “Great Balance” between learning content and game-based interactions.
