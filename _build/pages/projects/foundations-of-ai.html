The challenge

As the company rolled out Microsoft Copilot as its enterprise AI tool, employees arrived with very different starting points: some excited, some skeptical, and many unsure what was safe to share. The organization needed a common foundation in AI literacy that built confidence and good judgment, not just tool clicks.

My approach

Start with plain language

The course opens with what AI is and the buzzwords people keep hearing, then asks “Why AI?” and, just as important, “Does this need AI?” Learners practice spotting real AI opportunities in their own work through a continuous improvement lens.

Build judgment about risk

Sections on AI solutions, open versus closed systems, a human-centered approach, AI’s impact, and common media concerns lead into a “Safe or Risky?” activity where learners decide how to handle realistic situations involving sensitive information.

Finish with a skill people can use today

A four-step process turns understanding into action: describe your need, ask for solutions, iterate, and generate your prompt. Knowledge checks reinforce the key terms, and a companion handout gives learners something to keep.

Part of a bigger campaign

The course anchored a wider AI literacy effort that included a Copilot learning portfolio, a live Copilot Prompting 101 session, AI Lunch & Learns, and AI Hub articles.

Results

  • 729 completions within about three months of its April 2026 launch.
  • The wider Copilot learning portfolio generated 361 completions, and a live Copilot Prompting 101 session drew 191 employees.
  • I led the companion AI Information Campaign, publishing practical AI content and setting up metrics for the AI Hub. In tracked 30-day periods, the hub reached up to 444 unique viewers and 2,286 site visits, with people spending an average of 2 minutes 31 seconds each.
  • The first three campaign articles I wrote drew 389 views in about two months.
  • Team members specifically called out how practical the examples, exercises, and takeaways were.
